\begin{abstract}
We give exact rational parameters satisfying all 47 numerical conditions
used by the current leading claimed APSP construction. They yield the
conditional bound $O(n^{2.9929479187094})$, improving the displayed
$O(n^{2.99294792})$ bound by $1.2906\times10^{-9}$ in its exponent.
This is a parameter and numerical-certificate refinement of the existing
algorithm. Its APSP consequence depends on that construction's written
algebraic and runtime proof. A Lean development proves the real numerical
certificate and its explicit asymptotic cost ledger; the complete challenge
word-RAM theorem remains outside this formalization. A standalone Python
checker using rational interval arithmetic also accompanies this note.
The numerical feasibility result is independent of floating-point optimization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and precise status}
The problem is deterministic all-pairs shortest paths in a directed graph
with $n$ vertices, polynomially bounded integer weights, and no negative
cycles. The challenge requires reachability and distance outputs, with
edge-presence and weight matrices as inputs. For each fixed input-magnitude
exponent, its word-RAM program must work at every sufficiently large word
width proportional to $\log n$~\cite{race}.

The baseline is the aligned Walsh embedding, polynomial query cache, and
composite-incidence reduction in the note by the GitHub author
\texttt{shreshth-s}~\cite{base}, building on
Alman--Vassilevska Williams~\cite{avw} and Majdoddin~\cite{majd}.
The pinned source revision is \texttt{8a21eaae8159}; its full commit
identifier is recorded in \texttt{parameters.json}.
The challenge classifies its exponent $2.99294792$ as \emph{Claimed}:
the accompanying Lean development uses a different cost model from the
required \texttt{EndStatement} model~\cite{race}.

This note proves the numerical feasibility of a new rational witness.
The companion Lean development establishes the real-logarithm certificate
and its mathematical cost-ledger bound. Its algorithmic corollary uses the
cited written lemmas as dependencies.
A separate AI-agent review during preparation checked the local
identity, Walsh normalization, cache counting, box decomposition, phase
costs, certification rule, and reductions to min-plus and APSP; no defect
was found in those examined steps. That review and finite checks do not
constitute a formal proof of the complete word-RAM program.

\section{Cost expressions inherited from the construction}
Write $\HH(x)=-x\ln x-(1-x)\ln(1-x)$ and define
\begin{align}
 C_K&=\frac{c\HH(1/c)}{\ln4}, & T&=c-1+C_K,\label{eq:T}\\
 G&=\frac{c\HH(1/c)-c\HH((1-\theta)/c)-\theta\ln4}{\ln2},\label{eq:G}\\
 q_1&=\frac{\HH(\theta)}{\ln2}, & q_2&=q_1+2\theta,
 & E&=T+q_1.\label{eq:q}
\end{align}
The underlying selected-entry data structure has preprocessing
$\softO(qND^E+qN^2D^{-G}+qND)$ and a specified-coefficient query cost
$\softO(qD^{q_1}+D^{q_2})$, where $q$ is the cyclic ring length.
The layout condition uses $T$, whereas cache formation uses $E$.

Let $R>S\ge1$ be fixed integers, and put
\[
 \eta=\frac bR,\qquad u=S\eta,\qquad
 v=(R-S)\eta=b-u,\qquad e=b+u.
\]
Here $n^b$ is the middle-block scale, $n^\eta$ is the candidate-prime
scale up to logarithmic factors, $u$ is the frozen folded-modulus
exponent, and $v$ is the final cyclic-modulus exponent.

At the final cyclic endpoint, the four active runtime savings below
exponent three are
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{eG-\eta,\quad u-eq_1-\eta,\quad b-eq_2-\eta,
 \quad 1+\frac u2-eE-\eta.}\label{eq:four}
\end{equation}
The matrix-formation and incidence savings are respectively
$1-b-u/2-\eta$ and $b-\eta$. The layout margin is
$1+u/2-eT$. The supplied checker also retains the upstream's
ordinary-box-encoder checks, even though the aligned cache includes
the needed constant coefficients.

\section{How the new witness was found}
First disregard the arbitrarily small fixed-stage overhead $\eta$ and
balance the four expressions in~\eqref{eq:four}. Equating the first
three gives
\[
 u=e(q_1+G),\qquad b=e(q_2+G),\qquad
 q_1+q_2+2G=1.
\]
The last equality follows from $b+u=e$ and is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 G+q_1+\theta=\frac12.\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
Equating the cache saving gives
\[
 e=\frac1{T+(q_1+G)/2}
   =\frac1{T+1/4-\theta/2},\qquad
 \delta_\infty=\frac{G}{T+(q_1+G)/2}.
\]
Thus the search can solve~\eqref{eq:balance} for $\theta$ at each $c$
and optimize one scalar objective. A high-precision stationary-point
calculation gives approximately
\begin{align*}
 c_*&=10.66550024406572898499,\\
 \theta_*&=0.06529782369872630990,\\
 \delta_*&=0.007052081290690653281665.
\end{align*}
These values guided the rational witness. They do not prove a global
optimum or rule out a better algorithm. The certificate below does not
depend on the optimizer or on the stationarity equations being solved
exactly.

\section{The exact rational certificate}
\begin{proposition}
Take the following decimals as exact rational numbers:
\begin{align*}
 c&=10.665500244065729,&
 \theta&=0.06529782369872631,\\
 b&=0.046046352103722105,&
 \delta&=0.0070520812906,\\
 R&=10^{12},& S&=768979037380.
\end{align*}
With~\eqref{eq:T}--\eqref{eq:q} and the exact stage identities above,
all 47 numerical inequalities checked in the upstream aligned-cache
certificate hold strictly for target saving $\delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The accompanying \texttt{verify\_certificate.py} evaluates each expression
using rational interval arithmetic. A separate Lean proof establishes the
same strict conditions over the actual real logarithm and entropy
expressions. The exact inputs are recorded in
\texttt{parameters.json}. Natural logarithms are enclosed by the
explicit series-and-remainder argument in Section~\ref{sec:checker}.
The following entries are conservative lower bounds, rounded downward
from the computed rational interval endpoints:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Final cost component & Certified saving lower bound\\
\midrule
Boxes & $0.0070520812906416578320069557$\\
Residual coefficient queries & $0.0070520812906224020591996589$\\
Stored-box coefficient reads & $0.0070520812906609134787173031$\\
Polynomial cache & $0.0070520812910502201558170774$\\
Ordinary box encodings & $0.0424607608066502381465516591$\\
Matrix formation & $0.9362493081384414675389326354$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each exceeds $\delta$. The smallest runtime gap is greater than
$2.24020591996589\times10^{-14}$. The final layout margin exceeds
$0.0354086795160085803145447033$.

For completeness, the checker verifies both endpoints of both phases.
In the scalar phase, $x\in[0,u]$, the five principal savings are
\begin{align*}
 b-x+(b+x)G-\eta,&\qquad b-(b+x)q_1-\eta,\\
 b-(b+x)q_2-\eta,&\qquad 1+b-x/2-(b+x)E-\eta,\\
 &1-3x/2-\eta.
\end{align*}
In the cyclic phase, $z\in[0,v]$, they are
\begin{align*}
 b-u-z+eG-\eta,&\qquad b-z-eq_1-\eta,\\
 b-eq_2-\eta,&\qquad 1+b-u/2-z-eE-\eta,\\
 &1-3u/2-z-\eta.
\end{align*}
Each is affine in its phase variable. Endpoint verification therefore
covers the full interval. The checker also verifies the geometric-tail
conditions, increasing-binomial-tail conditions, positivity conditions,
tile margins, and exact integer stage identities. There are 19 initial
checks and 28 endpoint checks. Full directed lower and upper bounds are
recorded in \texttt{certificate.json}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional algorithmic consequence]
Assuming the selected-entry oracle, composite-incidence reduction,
min-plus/APSP transfers, and word-RAM cost interpretation in the cited
written construction, the stated deterministic integer APSP problem is
solvable in $O(n^{2.9929479187094})$ time.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
All runtime terms are bounded by $n^{3-\delta-\varepsilon}$ times fixed
powers of $\log n$, for some fixed $\varepsilon>0$, by the strict
certificate. These logarithmic factors are absorbed in $n^\varepsilon$.
The fixed number of stages, rounding overheads, bit-scaling calls, and
APSP squarings are included in that accounting. Since
$3-\delta=2.9929479187094$, the asserted exponent follows under the
stated algorithmic dependencies.
\end{proof}

The improvement over the displayed baseline is exactly
\[
 2.99294792-2.9929479187094=0.0000000012906.
\]
This is an extremely small asymptotic refinement. The stage count is
a fixed $10^{12}$; both it and the tiny positive slack imply enormous
hidden constants and asymptotic thresholds. No practical speedup is
claimed.

\section{Why the checker is exact}\label{sec:checker}
For rational $1\le y\le2$, put $z=(y-1)/(y+1)$, so $0\le z\le1/3$.
The identity
\[
 \ln y=2\sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
\]
gives the rational enclosure
\[
 2\sum_{j=0}^{39}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
 \ \le\ \ln y\ \le\
 2\sum_{j=0}^{39}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
 +\frac{2z^{81}}{81(1-z^2)}.
\]
The upper remainder follows by replacing every omitted denominator by
81 and summing a geometric series. For an arbitrary positive rational
$x$, the checker finds an integer $k$ with $1\le x/2^k<2$, then applies
$\ln x=\ln(x/2^k)+k\ln2$, including the correct interval direction
when $k$ is negative. All subsequent arithmetic uses Python's
\texttt{Fraction}; division requires a strictly positive denominator
interval. A condition passes only if its lower interval endpoint is
strictly greater than its threshold.

Decimal output uses outward rounding and is not used to decide a check.
Explicit exceptions, rather than Python assertions, enforce the checks
even under optimized execution. The implementation has no third-party
dependencies. Running
\begin{verbatim}
python3 verify_certificate.py --json certificate.json
\end{verbatim}
recomputes all 47 inequalities and exact stage identities.

\section{Lean formalization}\label{sec:lean}
The repository's \texttt{lean/} directory contains a Lean 4.33.1
development with Mathlib pinned to revision \texttt{0df444a360ea};
the full dependency identifiers are in \texttt{lake-manifest.json}.
The \texttt{LogBounds} module proves seven rational enclosures of actual
\texttt{Real.log} values using Mathlib's positive atanh-series
theorems~\cite{mathlib}. These use 40 terms and the valid geometric
tail majorant $2z^{81}/(1-z^2)$, which is looser than the Python checker's
majorant but sufficient for the chosen endpoints. A proved range-reduction
identity then gives enclosures of $\ln2$, $\HH(1/c)$,
$\HH((1-\theta)/c)$, and $\HH(\theta)$ at precision $10^{-24}$.

The theorem \texttt{ApspBound.Analytic.analyticNumericalCertificate}
proves all 47 inequalities at the real cost expressions, with no assumed
logarithm bounds. The development also proves the exact stage identities
and a common strict reserve of $2\times10^{-14}$ in the inequalities.
Affine interpolation establishes the cost conditions throughout both
phases, including all fixed $R+1$ stage indices, without enumerating them.

The theorem \texttt{ApspBound.Ledger.refinedExponentBigO} proves, for
every fixed natural $k$, that the explicitly defined mathematical ledger
\texttt{completeCost k} is $O(n^{2.9929479187094})$. It includes a quadratic
scan, incidence bookkeeping, six costs at each stage, upward integer
rounding, and fixed logarithmic padding. This is a closed theorem about
that ledger. A correct word-RAM implementation and domination of its
actual execution time by the ledger remain separate proof obligations.

The development uses no admitted proofs or additional project axioms.
Its transitive axiom audit allows only the standard
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
The rational certificate uses kernel reduction; no native decision axiom
is used. After installing the pinned dependencies, run:
\begin{verbatim}
cd lean
lake build
lake env lean Audit.lean
\end{verbatim}
The repository includes complete setup instructions and an automated
workflow for the build and audit. The formalization and research note
are attributed to Joshua Wharton, QFEX; the adapted proof arrangement
and underlying algorithm retain the upstream attribution stated above.

\section{Other avenues examined and verification limits}
The search also examined removing channel entries from the polynomial
cache, partial channel caching, fixed-profile prefix caching, and larger
aligned local identities. The versions with complete cost expressions
did not improve the leading exponent. In particular, reconstructing a
channel from two ordinary forms reduces preprocessing but increases the
residual query exponent; optimizing that tradeoff returned the full
cache endpoint. These numerical searches are not impossibility results.

The fact that $I-P$ has bilinear rank two suggests a possible improvement
to high-box queries, but the necessary extra stored states lose the
ordinary box-tail saving in the straightforward construction. Replacing
the query's $4^t$ factor by $2^t$ without charging those states would
not be justified. A larger improvement needs a new lemma that improves
one or more of the four costs in~\eqref{eq:four}.

As corroboration of the inherited proof, three upstream finite checkers
were independently rerun: the embedding checker passed 352 original
matrix outputs; the cache checker passed 34 arbitrary signed-array
queries and 20 encoders; the integration checker passed 530 normalized
scalar and cyclic coefficients. These tests do not prove the
asymptotic construction. The Lean numerical and cost-ledger proofs do
not discharge the full word-RAM implementation and correctness obligations.
This repository records the refinement; it does not
constitute a submission to, or verification by, the challenge.
